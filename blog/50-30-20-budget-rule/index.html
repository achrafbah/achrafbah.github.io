The 50/30/20 Budget Rule: A Simple Way to Control Your Money

October 15, 2026

The 50/30/20 rule is one of the simplest budgeting methods. You split your after-tax income into three buckets:

Bucket Share What goes in it
Needs 50% Rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments
Wants 30% Dining out, subscriptions, travel, hobbies
Savings & debt 20% Emergency fund, investing, extra debt payments

Example

With a monthly take-home pay of $3,000: $1,500 for needs, $900 for wants and $600 for savings and extra debt payments.

How to start in 15 minutes

  1. Write down your monthly after-tax income.
  2. Review the last 2-3 months of bank statements and tag each expense as need or want.
  3. Compare your real percentages to 50/30/20.
  4. Pick one category to adjust this month, usually wants.
  5. Automate the 20%: set a transfer to savings the day you are paid.

When the rule needs adjusting

  • High cost of living: needs may be 60-70%. Reduce wants first, then aim to grow income.
  • Heavy debt: shift to 50/20/30 (more to debt, less to wants) until the debt is gone.
  • Irregular income: base the budget on your lowest typical month.

Bottom line

No budget is perfect, but one you follow beats a perfect one you ignore. Start simple, track for a month, and refine.
